# Internal appeals

## The three steps

1. File a claim. A claim is a request for coverage, usually filed by you or your provider to get reimbursed for treatment or services.
2. Receive a denial. Your insurer must explain the denial in writing, within these timeframes:
   - 15 days for a request for prior authorization
   - 30 days for services you already received
   - 72 hours for urgent care
3. File an internal appeal. You can use your insurer's forms or write a letter with your name, claim number, and health insurance ID. You can also submit supporting material, such as a letter from your doctor. Your state's Consumer Assistance Program can file the appeal for you.

Deadline: you must file your internal appeal within 180 days (6 months) of receiving the denial notice.

## Records to keep

- Explanation of Benefits forms or letters describing the denial
- A copy of your appeal request
- Any supporting documents you sent, such as doctor letters
- Any form authorizing someone else, such as your doctor, to file for you
- Notes from phone calls, including dates, times, and the names and titles of the people you spoke with

Keep your originals and send copies to the insurer.

## Denials that can be appealed

You can appeal if your plan won't cover all or part of a service you believe should be covered. Common reasons for denial include:

- The benefit isn't offered by your plan
- Your medical problem started before you enrolled
- The care came from an out-of-network provider or facility
- The service is considered "not medically necessary"
- The treatment is considered experimental or investigative
- You're no longer enrolled or eligible
- Your coverage is being revoked retroactively for alleged false or incomplete application information

## Appeal timelines

- Appeals for services not yet received: decision within 30 days
- Appeals for services already received: decision within 60 days
- After the process ends, the insurer must issue a written decision. If the denial stands, the decision must explain how to request an external review.

## Urgent situations

- If the standard timeline could seriously jeopardize your life or your ability to regain maximum function, you can request an expedited appeal.
- In urgent cases, you can request an external review at the same time as your internal appeal, without finishing the internal process first.
- Urgent appeals must be decided as quickly as your condition requires, and no later than 4 business days after the request is received. The decision may be given verbally but must be confirmed in writing within 48 hours.
