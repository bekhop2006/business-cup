# Know your medical bill rights when using insurance

This page covers most types of private health insurance in the United States.

## The No Surprises Act

This federal law took effect January 1, 2022. It protects you from unexpected out-of-network bills for:

- Emergency room visits
- Non-emergency care connected to a visit to an in-network hospital, hospital outpatient department, or ambulatory surgical center
- Air ambulance services

These protections apply if you have coverage through an employer (including a Federal Employees Health Benefits plan), the federal Health Insurance Marketplace, a State-based Marketplace, or other individual market coverage.

## Emergency room care

"Emergency room" includes hospital emergency rooms, independent freestanding emergency departments, and any hospital department where you might receive post-stabilization services.

In most cases, you can't be charged more for emergency services than the in-network cost-sharing rate. This applies to the hospital, the providers treating you there, and your air ambulance provider if you arrived by air. Some plans don't cover emergency care, so check with your insurer if you're unsure.

Cost-sharing is the portion of a medical cost you pay when using insurance. It can take the form of a copayment, deductible, or coinsurance.

Ground ambulances are not covered by the No Surprises Act billing protections. They can still charge out-of-network rates and cost-sharing, unless state law says otherwise.

### After an emergency room visit

You're generally protected from unexpected out-of-network bills for post-stabilization services, which are additional services needed to stabilize your condition. These may be provided in the emergency room or another hospital department. An out-of-network provider may ask you to sign a notice and consent form, which would waive these protections.

## Planned, non-emergency care

You may be treated by an out-of-network provider at an in-network facility. For example, your hospital may be in-network while an attending physician is not.

You're protected from out-of-network charges for care connected to a visit to an in-network:

- Hospital
- Hospital outpatient department: an area of a hospital that treats people who don't need hospitalization
- Ambulatory surgical center: a non-hospital facility for outpatient surgery, where you may stay a few hours or one night

You may lose these protections if you sign a notice and consent form. These protections don't cover other settings, such as a doctor's office that isn't a hospital outpatient department, or an out-of-network facility.

## Exceptions

- Ground ambulance services: generally not covered.
- Vision-only and dental-only insurance: these protections generally don't apply, but may apply if vision or dental benefits are part of your health plan.
- Short-term limited duration and health care sharing ministry plans: services aren't subject to these protections.
- Fixed indemnity excepted benefits plans (such as hospital indemnity insurance): services aren't covered by these protections.
- Medicare, Medicaid, Indian Health Service, VA Health Care, or TRICARE: these plans already offer some protection from unexpected out-of-network bills.

## Signing a notice and consent form may increase your costs

If you're scheduled for out-of-network care, a provider may ask you to sign a notice and consent form. Signing it means you agree to receive out-of-network care and give up protection from unexpected out-of-network bills. You will likely pay more than in-network rates.

If you don't sign, a provider or facility may refuse non-emergency or post-stabilization care. You might need to reschedule with an in-network provider. Signing is your choice.

The form includes an estimate of your out-of-network care costs, information about your surprise-bill protections, the option to waive those protections and pay more, and, for post-stabilization care, a list of in-network providers at the facility who can provide needed services.

A provider may ask you to sign for out-of-network post-stabilization services if you can travel to a nearby in-network provider without medical or emergency transportation and are able to give informed consent.

Rules providers must follow:

- Answer your questions about the form, in person or by phone.
- Provide the form separately from other paperwork, not hidden within it.
- Deliver it in your preferred format, on paper or by email.
- Make it available in the 15 most common languages spoken in the state where you received care, and provide an interpreter if needed.
- Give it to you in advance, with timing that depends on when your care was scheduled.

If a provider doesn't follow these rules, you can submit a complaint to the No Surprises Help Desk.

You shouldn't receive a form in these situations:

- In the emergency room: providers can't ask you to waive protections for emergency services at hospitals, hospital outpatient departments, or independent freestanding emergency departments (except for post-stabilization services).
- When no in-network provider can offer the needed item or service at the in-network facility.
- From assistant surgeons, hospitalists, and intensivists.
- For emergency-related items and services provided by emergency medicine providers or suppliers, anesthesiology, pathology, radiology, neonatology, or diagnostic services (including radiology and laboratory services).

Questions before signing? Call the No Surprises Help Desk at 1-800-985-3059.

## When your provider leaves your health plan's network

If you have most types of health insurance, you may be eligible for up to 90 days of in-network coverage after your provider leaves the network. These "continuing care patients" include people who:

- Are undergoing treatment for a serious and complex illness
- Are receiving institutional or inpatient care
- Are scheduled for non-elective surgery
- Are pregnant and in treatment
- Are terminally ill

Ask your provider whether you qualify.

## If your provider isn't following these rules

Submit a complaint if you believe a provider isn't following the No Surprises Act. To complain by phone, call the No Surprises Help Desk at 1-800-985-3059.
