# Medical bill rights: the No Surprises Act

The No Surprises Act is a federal law that went into effect on January 1, 2022. It applies to most types of health insurance, and protects you from unexpected out-of-network medical bills from:

- Emergency room visits
- Non-emergency care related to a visit to an in-network hospital, hospital outpatient department, or ambulatory surgical center
- Air ambulance services

If you use health insurance, the Act protects you from unexpected out-of-network medical bills for emergency room visits.

Usually, if you don't have or use health insurance, providers must give you a good faith estimate of what your care will cost. You get the estimate when you schedule care in advance (at least 3 business days ahead) or if you ask for one. You may be able to dispute your bill if it's at least $400 more than the estimate.

You can submit a complaint if you believe that your facility, provider, or insurer isn't following these rules.
